    04

    The sound system

    Phonology studies how a language organizes sounds into meaningful patterns.

    Phoneme and contrast

    A phoneme is a sound category that can distinguish words. In English, /p/ and /b/ contrast in pat and bat. A minimal pair differs in one sound and demonstrates that contrast.

    Allophones and distribution

    Allophones are context-dependent pronunciations of one phoneme that do not change word meaning. Their distribution may be predictable from neighboring sounds, or vary across speakers.

    Syllables and sound patterns

    A syllable is commonly described as an onset, a nucleus and an optional coda. Languages differ in which consonant sequences they allow at syllable edges.

    Stress, rhythm and intonation

    Prosody organizes speech above the individual sound: stress marks relative prominence, rhythm patterns timing, and intonation uses pitch movement to shape phrasing and meaning.

    Common sound processes

    Assimilation makes a sound more like a neighbor; deletion removes a sound; insertion adds one; and neutralization removes a contrast in a particular context. These patterns vary by language and speaking style.
